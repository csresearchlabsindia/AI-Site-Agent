What ASA is

A four-wheel skid-steer unit on an Arduino UNO Q that patrols a construction site, checks that hard hats are actually on heads using an OAK-D depth camera, speaks to workers in the site language, records evidence, and reports its own state — including battery and e-stop — to a web dashboard. Nothing about its ability to stop depends on Linux being healthy.

Power

One 3S pack, two rails. The fuse and kill switch sit before the split so they protect everything; the e-stop sits after the split so it kills drive power only and leaves the robot able to say it was stopped. The compute rail is a 3 A buck feeding the UNO Q through its USB-C PD adapter, plus the OAK-D and the hub.

Rail sense: e-stop detection and battery gauge

The e-stop opens the motor rail. Reading that rail tells the MCU two things at once: whether the mushroom is pressed, and what the pack voltage is. Three resistors do it.

Calculation

V_A0 = V_rail × 9.91 / (32.6 + 9.93 + 9.91) = V_rail × 0.1890
Meter check: 2.37 V / 12.74 V = 0.1860  (−1.6 %, meter tolerance)
Firmware:   pack_V = counts × 3.3/1023 / 0.1860 = counts × 0.017340

Worst case, 5 % parts, 12.6 V:  2.58 V at A0   (limit 3.3 V)

Timing

The two BTS7960 input capacitors (660 µF total) can only drain through the divider's 53 kΩ once S1 opens: τ ≈ 35 s. Under drive the motors discharge them in milliseconds, so a press while moving is seen at once. A press while idle is seen about 15 s later; a 2 × 680 Ω bleeder across B+/B− (1.36 kΩ, 9 mA, 0.06 W each) would bring that under half a second and is on the roadmap. The 8 V threshold is chosen so the bleeder can be added without a firmware change.

A blown F1 is not detectable this way — it also takes the compute rail down. The symptom is simply a dark robot. On a USB-powered bench with no pack, A0 reads 0 V and the dashboard shows the hazard band; that is correct, the motors have no power.

RouterBridge handlers

Bridge.provide("set_status", set_status);  // LED face state 0–10
Bridge.provide("drive",      drive);       // signed PWM per side, −200…200
Bridge.provide("halt",       halt);
Bridge.provide("pack_v",     pack_v);      // float
Bridge.provide("estop",      estop_state); // bool

Every Bridge.call on the Linux side goes through one lock, because the 250 ms LED loop and the 10 Hz drive loop otherwise interleave frames on the single transport and the symptom is a random hang an hour into a demo.

UNO Q quirks that look like wiring faults

  • No pinMode() on PWM pins: it silently disables analogWrite.
  • Driver VCC on 3V3, not 5 V.
  • Bridge.update() does not exist in RouterBridge 0.4.3.
  • Reflash from SSH: arduino-app-cli app restart user:asa-mission-control compiles, flashes and restarts in one step.
